Energy-score UNet + Discrete Inverse Rendering

Plan doc — 2026-08-31. Draft after pivoting from DETR-slot to a stochastic dense predictor with an ILP post-solve. Written to align on the approach before committing code; the design questions in blue boxes are the ones we should resolve before implementation.

Why we're pivoting

The DETR-slot pipeline hits a ceiling on real videos (see MEMORY.md — sim→real gap is representational, no finetune has beaten algae_unet_4slot_v4 yet). Two independent insights motivate the pivot:

  1. The real–sim gap is largely photometric noise. After temporal-median subtraction, real videos are mostly the flagellum residual plus preprocessing artifacts (see figure below). The residual is what the model actually needs to explain — not the raw video, not a canonicalised band-passed thing.
  2. We don't need per-frame accuracy, we need recall × diversity. Each real frame has O(1–3) flagella; if the model outputs 100 candidates per clip, one landing close is enough for the downstream ILP (discrete_linking_opt) to lock onto it. That reframes the loss from "predict the flagellum" to "cover the flagellum with samples".

Step 1 — median-subtract everything (real and sim)

Every real video is preprocessed by global temporal-median subtraction (or sliding-window when the cell/pipette drifts). The sim's job is to match the appearance of that residual.

Step 2 — sim that matches the residual

The sim (JAX, batchable) produces a median-subtracted clip directly:

  1. Sample scene: cell bodies with contact-force repulsion; flagella attached to cell membranes (mixed polarity — sometimes darker, sometimes brighter than BG) and free-floating debris-flagella.
  2. Prescribe flagellum kinematics from a diverse mixture: curvature-wave (with tip amplification / breaststroke variants), near-static bent, and a lateral-sine baseline for coverage. Optionally: RFT swimming for free-floating flagella if we want moving objects — deeptangle's solve() is ~20 lines of JAX and gives calibrated low-Re propulsion.
  3. Render + apply per-clip distortions: faintness (contrast dim), defocus (Gaussian blur), motion blur (temporal box), drifting debris blobs, vignette, additive noise.
  4. Subtract the temporal median of the simulated clip itself. The model input distribution is the residual distribution — not the composite distribution.

A first cut of the sim lives at sim2real/sim/flagella_diverse.py (JAX, ~500 LOC, untested; needs one fix pass then a validation grid comparing sim residuals vs real residuals side-by-side).

Step 3 — UNet trained with the energy score

A UNet ingests the T-frame residual clip and outputs a stochastic prediction of flagellum parameters — a per-location skeleton with mixed existence. To turn the UNet into a sampler we condition on a per-pixel noise channel (simplest option; alternatives in the questions box below).

The energy score

For a predictive distribution P and target y, the energy score is

ES(P, y)  =  EX~P ‖X − y‖  −  ½ EX,X'~P ‖X − X'‖

It's a strictly proper scoring rule (Gneiting & Raftery 2007): minimized exactly when P is the true predictive distribution. The two terms have complementary jobs:

  • E‖X − y‖ — pulls samples toward the truth (accuracy).
  • −½ E‖X − X'‖ — rewards spread between independent samples (calibrated diversity). Without it, MSE-style training collapses to the conditional mean.

In practice we Monte-Carlo it with two independent forward passes per training example: draw X and X' with different noise seeds, compute the two terms on the mini-batch. Compute cost: 2× a normal forward.

What is X?

The concrete shape of X — a per-pixel heatmap? a set of K spline control points? PCA-reduced shape codes like deeptangle? — determines whether the energy score decomposes per-location or is a joint distribution over the whole scene. Called out in the questions box.

Step 4 — inference: draw many samples, pool with the prior

  1. Temperature-adjusted sampling. Scale the noise input by τ > 1 to inflate variance beyond training. Gives a variance floor so the model doesn't collapse to a modal answer on OOD real data. Draw N samples (say N = 64).
  2. Prior samples. Also draw M candidates from the sim itself (fresh simulator rolls, projected into the same parameter space). These act as a recall safety net: whenever the NN is silent in a location, the prior contributes hypotheses at the right scale.
  3. Pool = NN samples ∪ prior samples. Feed everything to DIR.

Step 5 — Discrete Inverse Rendering picks the coherent subset

discrete_linking_opt already solves this problem for worms/cells: an ILP (CP-SAT) picks a non-overlapping subset of primitives that (a) reconstructs the frames and (b) forms smooth tracks across time. We port the same machinery, with flagella as the primitive:

  • Same-frame constraint: flagella whose supports overlap more than a threshold can't both be selected.
  • Temporal constraint: selected flagella must chain into tracks; pay a birth/death cost for tracks that appear/disappear mid-clip.
  • Objective: reconstruction fit + track-smoothness + hypothesis score.

Two ingredients we get for free from the DIR side: gap-linking (bridge frame-to-frame blinks), and warm-start chaining (gap-1 → gap-2 → gap-3) for tractability.

End-to-end pipeline

Research question

Given the ILP downstream, we don't care about winning on any single prediction — we care about the answer being in the pool. The core question:

How do we maximise the chance that at least one sample (out of N) lands close to a real flagellum, when the real data is OOD relative to the sim?

The energy score is our first move — it explicitly rewards spread, so it should generalise better than MSE. Levers to explore later: sampling temperature, noise-channel shape, prior mixture weight, whether to also condition on a coarse feature from a frozen self-supervised backbone, whether to add an ensemble of UNets, etc.

Design decisions — resolved

Q1 ✓

Sample X = deeptangle-style grid of skeleton offsets

Per grid cell: (K spline control points as offsets, confidence). Energy score computed per cell independently. Scene-level joint calibration is DIR's job, not the NN's.

Q2 ✓

Stochasticity: noise-input channel, N independent draws (no AR)

Concatenate a low-frequency noise map z to the input; different seeds give different sample sets. At inference draw N independent samples with different z, pool everything into DIR — multi-modality comes entirely from the noise channel + the energy score's diversity term.

Why no AR: we considered a mask-and-repeat scheme (draw, subtract the top-scoring flagella from the residual, redraw). That gives disjoint-flagellum coverage, not multi-modality — different hypotheses about the same location would need a training-paradigm change (Attend-Infer-Repeat style). Not worth the complexity until we have evidence A.1 undercovers.

Escalation path (if A.1 undercovers real data):

  1. Add greedy peel-off as an inference-time layer on top of A.1 (many independent chains, each with a couple of peel-off iterations). No retraining.
  2. If still insufficient, escalate to full AIR-style AR (one flagellum per call, chained). Retraining required.

Risk to watch: the UNet learning to ignore z. Mitigations: per-clip noise (not per-frame), sufficient noise-channel scale, and monitoring the ratio ½E‖X−X'‖ / E‖X−y‖ during training — if it collapses to 0 we know z is being ignored.

Q3 ✓

DIR consumes individual per-sample flagella

No cross-sample NMS before the ILP. Ultra-loose pooling is exactly the deeptangle worm recipe DIR was designed for.

Q4 pending

Sim residual only for MVP; DIR-outputs-as-pseudo-labels TBD

MVP trains on median-subtracted sim residuals only. Once we have a working pipeline, revisit whether to bootstrap a second training round using DIR outputs on real data as pseudo-labels (self-training loop). Not committing to this yet — needs the MVP recall numbers first.

Q5 ✓

Two metrics: pre-DIR sample recall + post-DIR precision/recall

Pre-DIR: max over samples of Chamfer-close-enough recall, averaged over the 59 painted flagella — a pure "does the NN cover it" number. Post-DIR: precision + recall of the selected subset — end-to-end number.

later

Things we're deliberately deferring

  • RFT swimming — only worth adding if free-floating flagella / whole-cell translation turn out to matter for validation recall.
  • Style transfer between sim BG and real BG — median subtraction should mostly moot this.
  • Multi-scale training (deeptangle's --nworms 5,10,50,100,150,200,250 trick) — add if density robustness fails.
  • Real-video self-supervised pretraining — orthogonal to the recall pivot.

Progress log

step 1 ✓

Median-cleaned all 16 annotated sequences, characterised the residual

Script: scripts/inspect_real_residuals.py. Outputs at runs/real_residuals/{panel,hist,stats}. Loaded N=200 evenly-spaced frames per sequence, subtracted a sliding window-21 median.

Calibration numbers ([0,1] intensity units, aggregate across 16 seqs):

  • signal peak (residual p99.9): min 0.02, median 0.045, max 0.44
  • drift energy (global − window median): min 0.0007, median 0.0019, max 0.014
  • baseline brightness (raw p50): min 0.36, max 0.73
  • noise σ: quantization-limited (~0.004); real per-pixel noise is sub-quantum in most sequences.
step 2 ✓ (first pass)

Smoke-tested the diverse sim + calibrated distortion knobs

Bugs found + fixed: config needed frozen=True for jax.jit hashability. Added two capabilities that were missing but visible in real:

  • Slow cell drift — per-cell linear velocity applied to centers per frame. Real cells wander ≲3 px over a few-second clip.
  • Pipette primitive — soft-edged dark bar entering from a random edge, present in ~50% of clips (matches real).

Tuned ranges after visual comparison: cell radius 8-35 px (was 12-60), cell amp 0.05-0.28 (was 0.10-0.35), flag amp 0.02-0.70 (was 0.10-0.55), debris count 0-5 (was 0-12), debris amp 0.005-0.06 (was 0.03-0.20), vignette max 0.10 (was 0.35).

Remaining sim gaps (not blockers for the UNet prototype):

  • Extreme tail (p99.99): sim maxes at 0.44, real reaches 0.75. Would need rare "hot spot" events or reducing final [0,1] clip.
  • Real has a much sharper peak at 0 than sim — sim spreads noise more uniformly. Not a coverage problem (sim covers everything real has), but the sim is "denser" in the moderate range.
  • No numerical noise-σ calibration yet — real σ is sub-quantum so MAD saturates. Would need std-of-diff on a bright BG patch to get a real number; deferring.
step 3 ✓

Prototype trained + energy loss stable

sim2real/model/unet_energy.py (fresh U-Net, 32×32 grid, n_suggestions=4, 22 outputs per prediction). sim2real/model/energy_loss.py implements E‖X−y‖ − β·½E‖X−X'‖ with:

  • Safe-norm (sqrt(sum²+ε)) — jnp.linalg.norm has infinite gradient at 0; matching curves used to NaN backprop.
  • Diversity ceiling — min(diversity, k·accuracy) stops the model gaming the reward.
  • Grad clipping (global norm 1.0), bounded outputs (tanh on dy/dx/theta/pca, softplus on width), σ-normalized PCA basis.
  • Photometric L1 supervision on width + amp for predictions within score_sigma_px of a GT flagellum, so DIR can render candidates back to pixels.

Two 50k-step runs on CPU (BS=8/12, ~13 min each). v2 uses MSE score loss; v3 uses BCE (MSE collapsed to always-predict-0 on the imbalanced positives).

step 4 ✓

DIR implemented (CP-SAT ILP with temporal smoothness)

sim2real/dir/ — three modules:

  • renderer.py: Gaussian-tube rasterizer + support mask.
  • build_problem.py: hypothesis pool → node costs (score-only or recon+score), same-frame at-most-one on high-overlap pairs (>40%), temporal links across gap ≤ 2 frames with skeleton-distance cost, per-track birth/death cost.
  • solve.py: CP-SAT model with x/y/birth/death variables, flow conservation (x = birth + Σincoming = death + Σoutgoing), link implications, at-most-one cliques.

Simplifications vs. reference (discrete_linking_opt/2_..._celegans.py): drop multilinear overlap interactions (rely on at-most-one), drop cell divisions (flagella don't split). Adaptations for flagella: linking + birth/death is the essential structure.

Recall on the 59 painted flagella

p25=6.4   p50=8.1   p75=13.5   p90=34.9   p95=59.8
≤5 px: 13   ≤8 px: 50 (48%)   ≤12 px: 72 (69%)   ≤20 px: 88 (85%)

The model produces candidates near the GT (69% within 12 px, real flagellum width ≈ 4 px) but often just outside the tight 8 px threshold. The dominant failure mode is n_gt=2 scenes (Chlamydomonas pairs) where the model's two suggestions per grid cell struggle to disentangle the pair.
