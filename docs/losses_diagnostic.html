AR losses — why sim recall is stuck

Diagnostic doc — 2026-09-02. We can't overfit v8's AR model on 8 sim clips: after 3000 steps, loss doesn't reach zero and rollout recall on the training set is only 0.27. This page steps through each loss term, shows an interactive demo of the pathology, and surveys literature alternatives.

Setup

The model is an autoregressive knot generator on a 128×128 canvas. It produces:

  1. Attachment head: a 16×16 grid × 6 suggestions × (dy, dx, score) = 1536 candidate attachment slots per clip. Each slot proposes a (y, x) point that could be the base of a flagellum, and a score indicating confidence.
  2. Knot generator: autoregressive per-step categorical over (Δangle, step_length). Given a chosen attachment and current tangent, predicts the next knot for 24 knots total.

Training uses three losses:

total = 5 * coord_loss + 5 * score_loss + 1 * knot_loss

1. score_loss — attachment confidence ← main pathology

score_loss = BCE( sigmoid(score),  exp(-d² / σ²) ),  averaged over all 1536 slots
  where d = distance from slot's predicted (y,x) to nearest GT attachment
        σ = 12 px  (on 128-canvas)

The BCE target is a Gaussian bump: 1 at a GT attachment, decaying to 0 at ~24 px away. On a 128×128 canvas with 1-3 GT flagella per clip, most slots have target ≈ 0 because their predicted position lands in empty space.

The problem: with 1536 slots all averaged equally, the sea of near-zero targets swamps the few positive ones. The model quickly learns "output low logit everywhere" and BCE parks at ~0.2 — you can see the flat green line in the overfit run.

Variants to test

  • hard-mask: only supervise slots whose grid-cell centre is within R = 16 px of a GT. Throws out most negatives so the positives dominate.
  • focal-BCE (γ=2): weight easy negatives less. Standard fix from RetinaNet-style dense detection.

2. coord_loss — attachment regression ← weak gradient

coord_loss = mean over GT of ( min-over-all-slots of || pred_pos - gt_att || )

Only one slot per GT gets a coord gradient — whichever happens to be closest. All other 1535 slots receive no coord signal.

Combined with the score problem, this is why we see coord=0.03 (the winning slot fits great) but recall=0.27 (the score head has no idea which of the 1536 slots is the winning one, so at inference we pick top-scored slots that happen not to be GT-close).

Variants

  • per-cell-in-radius: every slot within R of a GT regresses to that GT. Dense gradient. Every cell near a GT learns to point at it.

3. knot_loss — AR teacher-forced categorical

knot_loss = mean_k [ CE(angle_logits[k], gt_angle_bin[k])
                    + CE(step_logits[k],  gt_step_bin[k])  ]

Standard AR CE. Fits fine (0.012 after overfit). But it's teacher-forced — at each step the patch is centred on the ground-truth knot, not on the model's own prediction. This is the "exposure bias" problem of AR sequence models: at inference the model diverges from GT after a few knots, and none of the training loss sees this compounding error.

4. What "recall" actually means ← Chamfer inflates our numbers

Recall is not "any predicted point close to GT" — it's symmetric Chamfer, averaged in both directions:

Chamfer(pred, gt) = 0.5 * ( mean_over_pred_pts( min_dist_to_any_gt_pt )
                          + mean_over_gt_pts  ( min_dist_to_any_pred_pt ) )

A rollout is close to a GT only when both:

  • Every predicted knot is on average near some GT point (pred→gt term — penalizes over-extension past GT end)
  • Every GT point is on average near some predicted knot (gt→pred term — penalizes under-coverage)

Recall per clip:

  1. Sample all rollouts (currently 1792 with wide-TTA).
  2. For each GT flagellum: compute Chamfer to every rollout, take the min.
  3. GT is "covered" if min-Chamfer ≤ 6 px (on 128 canvas ≈ 12 px on 256).
  4. Recall = covered / total across all clips.

Under the strict ordered-knot L2 (resample both to K=25 knots, match knot-i to knot-i, min over reverse) — the champion's actual recall is 0.673, not 0.99. The Chamfer number was inflated by the very snaking / partial-match failure mode we suspected. Ordered_L2 forces a knot-to-knot correspondence so a rollout that overlaps GT in the middle but misaligns at endpoints gets full penalty.

Under symmetric Hausdorff (max of directed max-of-mins) the strictest number: 0.24 @ 6 px. Only ~24% of GTs have a rollout where every predicted point is within 6 px of some GT point AND vice versa.

Chamfer's percentiles: p50=2.5, p99=5.8. ordered_L2: p50=5.1, p99=10.7. Real alignment error is ~2× the Chamfer number. Reference: sim2real/eval_v2/ordered_metric.py.

Length-mismatch penalty. The model always emits exactly 25 knots of step-length 0.75–4.25 px, so all rollouts are 18–102 px long. But 30% of real GT flagella are shorter than 18 px. A too-long rollout that mostly traces a short GT still incurs a large pred→gt term because the extra knots hang out in empty space. That's the mechanism by which the metric penalizes our no-STOP-token model on short flagella.

5. The loss → recall gap

Even if all 3 losses fit perfectly, the sampled rollout might still miss GT because:

  1. Score doesn't rank attachment slots by GT-proximity (see §1).
  2. Even the "best" attachment is a categorical sample from the model's score distribution — if it's diffuse we don't pick the right seed.
  3. Teacher-forced knot loss doesn't measure rollout Chamfer — a small early error compounds over 24 knots. Same exposure-bias problem as MLE-trained language models.

6. Overfit-to-1.0 with label smoothing

Adding knot_label_smoothing = 0.1 to the AB recipe closes the overfit gap: recall reaches 1.000 at step 500 and hits 1.0 six more times across 10k steps (interleaved with 0.87–0.93 dips).

5. What the literature does for similar problems

Dense detection heads

  • FCOS (Tian et al. 2019) — per-pixel classification + regression of a bounding box. Uses centerness to soften the "which pixel is correct" ambiguity, and only regresses inside the object. This is exactly the per-cell-in-radius coord variant.
  • CenterNet / CornerNet — Gaussian heatmap targets at object centres (like our score target) but with focal-loss variants to re-balance the positive/negative extreme imbalance. Zhou et al. show mAP jumps from 28→39 by swapping BCE→focal on identical targets.
  • DETR (Carion et al. 2020) — set prediction with Hungarian matching. Only N=100 slots (not 1536) and each slot must own at most one GT. Removes the negative-slot swamping entirely.

Sequence / spline losses

  • Chamfer distance — symmetric mean-of-mins between two point sets. Standard for point-cloud generation. Robust to point ordering, no need for knot correspondence, differentiable everywhere. Would let us optimize rollouts directly instead of teacher-forced knots.
  • Soft-DTW (Cuturi & Blondel 2017) — differentiable dynamic time warping. Softmin-min over all alignments of two sequences. O(K²) per pair. Good when curves have varying lengths (our exact problem — sim vs real length distributions differ). Used for time-series generation, speech, handwriting.
  • Sinkhorn / Wasserstein — optimal transport between point sets as a differentiable soft-Chamfer. Slightly slower than Chamfer but handles cardinality mismatch. Used in DeepTangle and many neural-implicit-curve papers.
  • Frechet distance — "man walking a dog on a leash" — max of min-distances along a common time parameterisation. Not commonly differentiable but robust; used in trajectory clustering.

AR sequence models specifically

  • Scheduled sampling (Bengio et al. 2015) — mix teacher-forced and model-generated context during training to close the exposure-bias gap. Cheap to implement.
  • DAgger / student-forcing — always use model-generated context; re-label the ground truth to the closest possible extension. Common in imitation learning of trajectories.
  • REINFORCE with Chamfer reward — sample a full rollout, back-prop through the categorical with the rollout's Chamfer-to-GT as reward. High variance but directly optimizes the metric.
  • Straight-through Gumbel-softmax — differentiable sampling for the categorical; lets us backprop through the whole 24-step rollout. Applicable but often numerically fragile at long horizons.

Application-specific

  • DeepTangle (already in the codebase reference dir) — predicts multi-instance splines end-to-end using a Sinkhorn set-loss + spline L2. No teacher-forcing.

Why v14/v15 didn't win

Scheduled sampling was expected to fix exposure bias by mixing self-context into training. In practice v14 peaked lower than v13 (0.77 vs 0.85 at step 5k). Hypothesis: SS active from step 0 hurts the initial categorical formation. v15 delayed SS to step 20k but was killed for v16 before it completed.

v16 design

Three simultaneous changes attacking three separate failure modes:

  1. 12 knots instead of 24: exposure-bias compounding grows roughly linearly with rollout length. Half the steps → half the error accumulation. Doubled step_max (4.5 → 6.0) and doubled bin range (n_step_bins 8 → 10) to keep coverage of the same 12–72 px length range that we used to hit with 24 × [0.75, 4.25].
  2. Stop token (variable-length rollout). Currently 30% of real GT flagella are shorter than the model's minimum (18 px). Chamfer inflates recall by ~2× on those (§4). Fix:
    • Determine training-time knot count from GT arc length: k_real = ceil(arc_length / target_step_px), clipped to [1, 12].
    • Resample GT to k_real+1 uniform-arc points, pad to 13 with the last point.
    • Knot CE masked to valid_mask = arange(K) < k_real so padded knots don't wash out the signal.
    • Stop head predicts a per-knot logit; stop_target[k_real-1] = 1, else 0. Rollout terminates at the first knot where sigmoid(stop) > 0.5.
  3. Smoothness prior on the categorical. Currently the knot CE treats every step independently — two adjacent knots can each hit CE=0 while predicting wildly different Δangles. Real Chlamydomonas beats are smooth. New loss term: 
    sm_loss = mean_k [ (E[Δang_k] − E[Δang_{k-1}])² ]
where  E[Δang_k] = Σ_i softmax(angle_logits[k])_i · bin_center_i
    Encourages the model to make similar predictions at adjacent knots. Weight 0.3 in v16.
